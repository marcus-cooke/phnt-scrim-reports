Four preparation themes

Black Sails / two recordingsRecurring closing intent

Preserving scoring bodies can recover a deficit; execution still decides the finish

EvidenceThe first Black Sails recording recovers from 84–98–99 to a 100–98–99 win. The second again emphasises revives, smoke and surviving bodies, reaches 99–72–99, then loses 99–72–100.

PHNT practiceRun a final-point scenario with a defended regroup area, a protected APC and one assigned recovery route. Test whether the team can hold scoring presence while replacements arrive.

Two matches support recurring intent. They do not show an improvement trend, a guaranteed counter or identical patch, rules and roster conditions.

First match: deficit and recovery · Second match: survival calls · Second match: completed loss

Supply and reinforcementDifferent roles, shared dependency

The next delivery or spawn can matter as much as the current firefight

EvidenceAdversary’s Humvee crew scouts logistics and supply corridors. The WOUF builder identifies a mechanical pallet delivered instead of building supplies. Black Sails’ spawn UI explicitly reports its vehicle radar destroyed, followed by a recovery problem in comms.

PHNT practiceAssign supply type, route security, repair owner and a fallback spawn before the push. Practise a wrong-pallet correction and an APC radar failure while the scoring group stays active.

These are distinct mechanisms. No shared resource-loss rate or causal points value is measured.

Supply-route scouting · Wrong supply category · Disabled spawn radar

FortificationDefence versus movement

A useful fort needs sightlines, warning and an exit

EvidenceWOUF hardens Tower 2 but discusses roof pressure, a reduced garrison and an access problem. Adversary spends time demolishing a captured site while comms say players need to be in point. Black Sails requests jump-ups and anti-air around its own covered position.

PHNT practiceInspect a build from the defender’s eye level. Test roof warning, friendly entry and exit, then time how quickly the nonessential garrison can move to the scoring area.

The tower and FOB layouts differ. A strong wall is not treated as evidence of an impenetrable base, and the exact cost of staying is unknown.

Tower access trade-off · Leaving the tower · Demolition competes with scoring · Defensive visibility

Air and command coordinationSilk competitive section only

Target focus needs a working command path and separated approach lanes

EvidenceThe pilots report missing command whispers, request warning before a drill pull and coordinate artillery against rooftop AA. Their paired response later includes a collision. The final result is an Amnesty win, 100–94–40.

PHNT practiceVerify a two-way command-to-air check, a persistent target mark, artillery ownership, lead/trail order and a common pass-exit direction.

The artillery effect is principally reported by the pilots; it is not an isolated causal test. Earlier public play is excluded from the competitive dossier.
